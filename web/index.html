A security app that can't phone home.

Mume checks your phone for the things that actually get people robbed — cloned banking apps, hidden permissions, intercepted traffic, breached passwords. Then it tells you exactly what it found and exactly what it could not see.

It does all of it without an internet connection, because the app was never granted one.

What it checks

The things that actually cost people money.

Not a virus scanner. Phones do not get viruses the way computers did — they get cloned apps, over-permissioned apps, and hostile networks.

Highest signal · Android

An app pretending to be your bank

A repackaged app keeps the real one's identity but is signed with a different key. Mume carries the genuine signing keys for thousands of well-known apps and compares them on the device. Cloud scanners routinely miss this because they match file hashes, which a repacker changes for free. It is the single most common route to a stolen banking login on Android, and detecting it needs no server at all.

Network

Someone reading your encrypted traffic

An interception proxy has to install its own certificate authority on your device. Mume lists every certificate authority that did not ship with the OS — and tells you when it was your employer.

Apps · Android

Permission combinations, not permissions

Reading your texts is what a messaging app does. Drawing over other apps is what a chat head does. Controlling the screen is what a screen reader does. All three at once is a banking trojan, and almost nothing legitimate needs them together.

Passwords

Breached passwords, checked without sending anything

Two million of the most commonly breached passwords ship inside the app as a compact filter. Your password is tested against it on the phone. Other tools send a hash prefix to a server; Mume sends nothing, so there is no request to log, correlate or subpoena.

Personal data

Photos that carry your home address

Most photos record the exact coordinates where they were taken. Messaging apps usually strip that; email attachments and many platforms do not. Mume finds them and can write location-free copies without touching the originals.

Scoring

A score you can audit

Every point deducted traces to one finding, the per-finding numbers sum exactly to 100 − score, and tapping the score shows the arithmetic. Categories are capped so one bad area cannot make an otherwise sound phone read as a disaster, and repeat findings decay so ten small issues never outweigh one serious one. None of that is hidden — it is in the app, on a screen we called “These add up. Check us.”

macOS & Linux

Not offered yet, deliberately. The macOS collector has never been run on a Mac and the Linux one has only run under WSL. Both are written to report “not checkable” rather than guess, so they would be safe — and nearly useless. Build from source if you want to try them.

Verify it before you run it

A security tool asking to be trusted should be the easiest thing in the world to check. Compare the hash of what you downloaded against the one published here.

Windows will warn you. Here is exactly why.

You will see “Windows protected your PC”, and you may need to click More info → Run anyway. That is SmartScreen, and it is behaving correctly: this build is not code-signed. A signing certificate costs a few hundred pounds a year, and Mume has not bought one.

We are not going to tell you to ignore a security warning — that is the habit every piece of malware relies on. What we will do is give you a way to check instead of trusting: the hashes above, and the source below. If the hash matches what is published here and here matches what is in the repository, you know what you have. If either does not, do not run it.

Mume is free and AGPL-3.0. No account, no telemetry, no paid tier. If someone ships a modified Mume, the licence obliges them to publish their changes — which is the only way “you can verify this” survives a fork.

What Mume does not have

No account. No server. No SDK.

There is nothing to sign up for, because there is nothing to sign up to. No analytics library, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising ID, no third-party code of any kind in the app. A security app that ships someone else's telemetry has lost the argument before it starts.

The trade-off

What we give up for it

Threat data ships inside the app and refreshes when the app updates, so it is days old rather than minutes old — Mume shows you the date on every scan. Shortened links cannot be followed, so they are reported as unknown, never as safe. And because we collect nothing, we genuinely do not know how many people use which feature.

Free

All of it, at no cost, with nothing to upsell

Every check is available to everyone. There is no paid tier, no advertising, and no data to sell — the architecture makes selling data impossible rather than merely against policy. If that sounds too good to be true, note that a product with no servers and no accounts costs almost nothing to run.
